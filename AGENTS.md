## Writing and editing text

Whenever you create or revise text, headings, descriptions, or paragraphs, make
the wording sound as natural and personal as possible. Treat this as a required
review step before finishing any content change.

- Match Raul's voice: direct, plainspoken, specific, and free of marketing copy.
- Prefer concrete details about what he built, did, or learned over generic
  statements about passion, innovation, expertise, or impact.
- Remove filler, repeated ideas, canned transitions, buzzwords, exaggerated
  claims, and wording that sounds like an AI-generated portfolio template.
- Avoid stock formulas such as "not just X, but Y," forced three-item lists,
  and unnecessary em dashes. Use the sentence structure the idea needs.
- Keep useful technical details, but explain them in ordinary language.
- Never invent experiences, results, or running services to make copy stronger.
  Distinguish completed work from plans.
- Read the final copy as something Raul would say aloud. Rewrite anything that
  feels stiff, inflated, impersonal, or redundant; delete it if it adds nothing.
